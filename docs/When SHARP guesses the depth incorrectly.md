I call this Single-View Geometry-Refined Gaussian Splatting (SGR-GS). It operates strictly in isolation and will only activate when the user toggles it on.The Mathematical Diagnosis: Why SHARP FailsIn a standard multi-view Gaussian Splatting pipeline, geometry is constrained by overlapping camera rays. In monocular models like SHARP, the network minimizes a generalized loss over a dataset, meaning your specific image's high-frequency details and exact depth map are smoothed out. The model attempts to "fake" missing data by drastically scaling up the covariance matrix ($\Sigma$) of background splats, causing the ugly, stretched artifacts when you shift the FOV.The Solution: WebGPU Test-Time Optimization (TTO)We will introduce a highly optimized, short-burst rendering-and-refinement loop (approx. 100–200 iterations) entirely inside a dedicated WebGPU compute worker. 
This process will take the raw SHARP .ply output, lock onto the original 2D image, and force the Gaussians to align perfectly to the ground truth.1. Photometric Loss (For Pixel-Perfect Front)We must force the rendered image $I_{render}$ to perfectly match the original high-resolution input image $I_{GT}$. We optimize the spherical harmonics ($c$) and opacities ($\alpha$) using a combined $L_1$ and Structural Similarity (SSIM) loss:$$\mathcal{L}_{photo} = (1 - \lambda) \| I_{render} - I_{GT} \|_1 + \lambda (1 - \text{SSIM}(I_{render}, I_{GT}))$$This guarantees that from the exact front (the input FOV), the rendering is indistinguishable from the original photograph.2. Scale-Invariant Depth Regularization (Fixing 3D Placement)To correct the inaccurate 3D spatial placement, we extract a high-quality relative depth map $D_{GT}$ from the input image (using a lightweight ONNX/WebNN model like Depth Anything V2 in the background). We then calculate the expected depth of our Gaussians $D_{render}$ via alpha-compositing:$$D_{render} = \sum_{i=1}^N \mu_{z,i} \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$$Because monocular depth is relative, we cannot use direct absolute error. Instead, we use a Scale-Invariant Depth Loss (Pearson correlation) to align the structural topology of the splats:$$\mathcal{L}_{depth} = \left\| \frac{D_{render} - \text{mean}(D_{render})}{\text{std}(D_{render})} - \frac{D_{GT} - \text{mean}(D_{GT})}{\text{std}(D_{GT})} \right\|_2^2$$This physically pulls the $\mu_z$ coordinates of the splats into their correct Z-space locations without stretching them.3.
 Pixel-Aware Density Control (Handling the 1M/3M Splat Limits)The reason your 3M splat option looks bad is because standard 3DGS clones splats based on the average gradient magnitude from observable views. For monocular generation, this creates "floaters" near the camera.We will implement a Pixel-Aware Gradient (based on 2024/2025 research like Pixel-GS). We weight the gradient by the number of pixels $p_i$ the Gaussian covers:$$\nabla_{\mu} \mathcal{L}_{weighted} = \frac{1}{\sum p_i} \sum p_i \nabla_{\mu} \mathcal{L}$$If a background splat is massive and covering too many pixels, this forces the system to split it into smaller, accurate splats, strictly separating the foreground from the background.
